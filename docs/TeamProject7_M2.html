Part 1 — Agent Prototype v1

1.1 Prototype summary & repository

FinePrint Agent is a single-tool agent that accepts an uploaded lease or contract PDF and returns a ranked list of money-loss risks. Every finding must include a verbatim quote and page number. Findings whose quotes cannot be located in the parsed document text are dropped (fail closed).

The M2 vertical slice is implemented as a TypeScript monorepo on branch agent-architecture:

  • apps/web — Next.js upload UI and scan APIs
  • packages/shared — shared contracts (RiskFinding, scan statuses)
  • packages/mcp-document — MCP tool document_parse
  • packages/agent — provider wrapper, persona, orchestrator, citation validator, local store
  • supabase/migrations — Postgres + RLS schema for the cloud path
  • tests/ — graded pipeline tests including a hallucination trap

Model stack (unchanged from M1): primary NRP open LLM (gpt-oss, OpenAI-compatible), fallback Anthropic via MODEL_PROVIDER, and mock for offline teammate onboarding and CI.

1.3 Agent persona, MCP integration, and citation guardrail

Persona / core instruction. The agent is a document stipulation and money-loss scanner. It must ignore any instructions embedded in the PDF (prompt-injection resistance), must not invent fees or dates, and must not offer legal advice or enforceability opinions.

MCP tool. document_parse converts a PDF into page-mapped plain text suitable for citation checks. The same parser is importable by the orchestrator and exposable as a stdio MCP server (npm run mcp:document).

Output schema. Structured RiskFinding objects: risk_type, severity, plain_english, quote, page, optional character offsets.

Guardrail. After model (or mock) extraction, validateCitations requires each quote to be a normalized substring of the corresponding page text. Ungrounded findings never reach the UI.

1.5 Known limitations (honest M2 scope)

  1. Sync path: demo-sized PDFs process inline; very large files may need an async worker (planned).
  2. Mock provider: offline/CI uses keyword heuristics; live demos should set MODEL_PROVIDER=nrp.
  3. No legal advice: quotes and plain-English risk labels only—not enforceability or negotiation counsel.
  4. Auth UI deferred: M2 uses a demo owner id locally; Supabase RLS migrations are ready for M3.

Positioning. Closest substitutes are new AI lease apps; the default free substitute is ChatGPT. FinePrint’s wedge is a document-agnostic money-loss scanner with mandatory quote grounding and no legal advice— thinner and more honest than “AI lawyer,” more structured than ChatGPT paste.

2.2 Market size + opportunity

Total Addressable Market (TAM). FinePrint sits at the intersection of (a) people who sign consequential consumer/housing documents without counsel and (b) software that helps review those documents with AI. On the demand side, U.S. renter households alone were about 46.2 million in Q3 2025 (34.7% of occupied units), per Census Housing Vacancies and Homeownership summaries reported in industry coverage; Arbor’s 2025 update similarly places rental households near a record 46.1 million. That housing base is only the lease wedge—students and early-career adults also sign gym, phone, insurance, loan, and internship agreements. On the software side, analyst estimates for AI / contract-review platforms vary by definition, but converge on a multi-billion-dollar category growing quickly: e.g., AI contract review valued around USD 2.8B in 2025 with a projected USD 3.4B in 2026 (NextMSC), while MarketsandMarkets estimates legal AI software near USD 3.11B in 2025 with ~28% CAGR through 2030. We treat TAM as the broad consumer-plus-SMB “understand what I’m signing before money is lost” opportunity spanning those renter/signer populations and the AI contract-review spend envelope—not the entire USD ~1.9T U.S. rental housing industry (Market Data Forecast), which overstates what software can capture.

Serviceable Addressable Market (SAM). Within two years, an AI agent like FinePrint can realistically reach English-speaking U.S. students and young adults who (1) can upload a PDF, (2) will not pay $150–$650 for a lawyer for every document, and (3) already experiment with ChatGPT on contracts. Practically, that is a slice of the ~46M renter households plus adjacent consumer-contract signers—starting with university-dense metros and online distribution (Vercel URL, campus channels). We do not claim enterprise legal-department budget; SAM is consumer self-serve pre-sign review, not AmLaw workflow replacement.

Why now. Three shifts in the last 2–3 years make this acute and solvable: (1) LLMs with usable long-context and tool calling make clause extraction cheap enough for free/near-free student use via NRP; (2) housing affordability keeps renter formation elevated relative to ownership, so more people face long leases under time pressure; (3) public awareness of AI hallucination risks creates demand for products that cite the source document rather than free-floating summaries—exactly FinePrint’s guardrail.

Biggest market risk (not technology). Trust collapse from a single invented fee, or substitution by “good enough” free ChatGPT, could prevent conversion even if extraction quality is decent. Related risk: users (or regulators) perceive any money-loss scanner as unauthorized practice of law if messaging drifts into advice. Our mitigation is product honesty—quotes only, no enforceability claims—and fail-closed citation validation.

Part 1 (continued) — How to run & what teammates own

1.6 Setup instructions (from repository README)

A teammate who did not write the code can reproduce the M2 prototype without cloud credentials:

  1. Clone https://github.com/krocks9903/FInePrint-Agent and check out agent-architecture.
  2. Copy .env.example to .env (default MODEL_PROVIDER=mock).
  3. Run npm install, then npm test, then npm run dev.
  4. Open http://localhost:3000, upload a PDF, and inspect ranked grounded findings.
  5. For live NRP demos, set NRP_BASE_URL, NRP_API_KEY, and MODEL_PROVIDER=nrp (values shared in the team GroupMe; never committed).

Optional cloud path: apply supabase/migrations/20260921_m2_init.sql, set Supabase keys, and switch STORAGE_BACKEND=supabase when ready. M2 demos may remain on the local JSON store under ./data (gitignored).

1.7 Request lifecycle (canonical)

  1. Upload — POST /api/scans accepts application/pdf only, enforces a size cap, stores bytes, creates a scans row (queued).
  2. Parse (MCP) — document_parse produces page-mapped text; pages are persisted for citation checks.
  3. Analyze — FinePrint agent extracts candidate RiskFinding[] via NRP/Anthropic/mock.
  4. Validate — deterministic substring check; ungrounded quotes excluded from the user list.
  5. Return — UI polls GET /api/scans/:id; response omits full page text (PII minimization).

Content-hash caching (SHA-256 + prompt version + model id) skips redundant LLM work when the same document is re-uploaded— important for demos and for cost control if the Anthropic fallback is active.

Part 2 (continued) — Competitive detail & implications

2.4 What we learned from competitors (for product scope)

Direct lease-AI products (LeaseScan, LeaseLenses, LeaseAI, Vikk) prove that “upload a lease PDF and get risks” is already a marketed category in 2026. That is useful validation and a warning: FinePrint will not win by claiming to be the first lease chatbot. Differentiation has to be crisp.

  • Against ChatGPT: structure + citation validator + money-loss ranking, not open-ended chat.
  • Against lease-only apps: document-type breadth (gym, phone, loan, internship) aligned with M1 interviews.
  • Against Vikk-style “attorney-grade” assistants: refuse enforceability and negotiation scripts in M2/M3 messaging to reduce UPL/trust risk.
  • Against LegalZoom / attorneys: position as a pre-screen, not a replacement—surface quoted risks so users know when escalation is worth $300+.

Competitor pricing bands also inform freemium design: consumer lease AI is marketed from roughly free–$12/month, while attorney review sits roughly $150–$650. FinePrint can live in the free-to-low-price band while inference runs on NRP, then introduce paid tiers only after M4 evidence that users return.

2.5 SAM sketch (transparent assumptions)

Illustrative—not a forecast we would pitch as audited financials. If even 1% of ~46M U.S. renter households used a consumer AI pre-sign tool once per year, that is ~460k annual scan events. At a hypothetical $5 paid deep-scan (or equivalent freemium conversion), gross would be on the order of low millions—enough to matter for a student venture, tiny versus enterprise legal AI. The point for M2 is directionality: the renter base is large; willingness to pay is unproven and must be tested in M4; ChatGPT remains the free substitute that caps pricing power.

Open questions carried from M1 into M2/M3: Is the bottleneck finding clauses, understanding them, or acting before the deadline? How wrong can the agent be before trust dies? M4 user testing is designed to answer both with behavioral evidence, not surveys alone.

Conclusion

Milestone 2 delivers a real, running, single-tool agent with MCP document_parse, an architecture diagram that matches the code (Live vs Planned), automated tests including a hallucination trap, and a market package—competitive landscape with primary-source citations, TAM/SAM with Census and analyst sources, and a complete nine-block BMC draft that states uncertainties instead of leaving blanks.

The agent is not yet impressive on every document, and it is not a lawyer. It is honest about those limits. That honesty is the product strategy: grounded money-loss visibility before signing, with a path to multi-agent extraction and evaluation in M3/M4.
